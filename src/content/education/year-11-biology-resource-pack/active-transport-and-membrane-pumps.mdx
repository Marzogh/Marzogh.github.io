## Overview

A general lesson on ATP-dependent transport, movement against gradients, and key membrane pump examples.

## Learning Goals

- distinguish active from passive transport
- explain why ATP is required
- apply pump function to physiological examples

## Worked Example

In the sodium-potassium pump, three Na+ ions are exported and two K+ ions imported per ATP hydrolyzed, maintaining gradients critical for nerve impulse transmission.

## Check For Understanding

- What does against the gradient mean?
- Why is ATP needed in active transport?
- How do ion gradients support cell function?

## Common Misconceptions To Address

- active transport is faster diffusion
- ATP is optional for membrane pumps
- pumps move ions randomly

## Teaching Sequence (Suggested)

1. Start with retrieval questions from prior lessons.
2. Model one example step by step using scientific vocabulary.
3. Run guided practice with immediate feedback.
4. Move to independent application.
5. End with an exit task that requires evidence-based reasoning.

## Adaptation Notes

- Support: sentence starters, partially completed diagrams, key-term banks.
- Extension: unfamiliar data, compare-and-justify prompts, evaluate limitations.
- Assessment alignment: include one short-response and one extended-response item.
