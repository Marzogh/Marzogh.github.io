## Overview

A general lesson on action potentials, synaptic transmission, and factors affecting signal speed and reliability.

## Learning Goals

- describe resting and action potentials
- explain saltatory conduction and myelination
- outline chemical synapse signaling steps

## Worked Example

Depolarization opens voltage-gated sodium channels; repolarization follows potassium efflux; refractory period helps ensure one-way impulse propagation.

## Check For Understanding

- Why does myelin increase conduction speed?
- What happens at a chemical synapse?
- How does refractory period affect signal direction?

## Common Misconceptions To Address

- impulses travel as neurotransmitters inside axons
- larger stimulus always creates larger action potential amplitude
- synapses are electrical only

## Teaching Sequence (Suggested)

1. Start with retrieval questions from prior lessons.
2. Model one example step by step using scientific vocabulary.
3. Run guided practice with immediate feedback.
4. Move to independent application.
5. End with an exit task that requires evidence-based reasoning.

## Adaptation Notes

- Support: sentence starters, partially completed diagrams, key-term banks.
- Extension: unfamiliar data, compare-and-justify prompts, evaluate limitations.
- Assessment alignment: include one short-response and one extended-response item.
