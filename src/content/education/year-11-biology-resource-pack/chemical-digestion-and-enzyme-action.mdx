## Overview

A reusable lesson on enzyme-mediated digestion, substrate specificity, and absorption-ready products.

## Learning Goals

- map enzymes to substrates and products
- explain digestive conditions across gut regions
- apply digestion concepts to food composition examples

## Worked Example

Amylase hydrolyzes starch into smaller sugars; proteases digest proteins to peptides/amino acids; lipases split lipids into fatty acids and glycerol.

## Check For Understanding

- Why is digestion both mechanical and chemical?
- Which enzymes act on carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids?
- How does pH affect digestion along the alimentary canal?

## Common Misconceptions To Address

- one enzyme can digest all macromolecules
- digestion ends in the stomach
- absorption and digestion are the same process

## Teaching Sequence (Suggested)

1. Start with retrieval questions from prior lessons.
2. Model one example step by step using scientific vocabulary.
3. Run guided practice with immediate feedback.
4. Move to independent application.
5. End with an exit task that requires evidence-based reasoning.

## Adaptation Notes

- Support: sentence starters, partially completed diagrams, key-term banks.
- Extension: unfamiliar data, compare-and-justify prompts, evaluate limitations.
- Assessment alignment: include one short-response and one extended-response item.
