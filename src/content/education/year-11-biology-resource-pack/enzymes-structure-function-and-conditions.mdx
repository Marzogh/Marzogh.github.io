## Overview

A general lesson on enzyme structure, specificity, and effects of temperature, pH, and substrate concentration.

## Learning Goals

- explain active site specificity
- interpret enzyme rate graphs
- predict effects of environmental change on activity

## Worked Example

When temperature rises to an optimum, enzyme rate increases due to higher kinetic energy and successful collisions. Beyond optimum, active-site shape changes and rate declines.

## Check For Understanding

- Why do enzymes have an optimum temperature and pH?
- What causes denaturation?
- How do you interpret plateau regions in rate curves?

## Common Misconceptions To Address

- enzymes are consumed in reactions
- higher temperature always means faster rate
- denaturation means the substrate changed

## Teaching Sequence (Suggested)

1. Start with retrieval questions from prior lessons.
2. Model one example step by step using scientific vocabulary.
3. Run guided practice with immediate feedback.
4. Move to independent application.
5. End with an exit task that requires evidence-based reasoning.

## Adaptation Notes

- Support: sentence starters, partially completed diagrams, key-term banks.
- Extension: unfamiliar data, compare-and-justify prompts, evaluate limitations.
- Assessment alignment: include one short-response and one extended-response item.
