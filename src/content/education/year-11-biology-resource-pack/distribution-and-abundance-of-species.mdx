## Overview

A general lesson on how abiotic and biotic factors shape where species occur and in what numbers.

## Learning Goals

- distinguish distribution from abundance
- relate environmental variables to species patterns
- use evidence to explain observed ecosystem variation

## Worked Example

If salinity increases along a transect, only tolerant species may persist at high-salinity points, producing a non-random distribution pattern tied to abiotic stress.

## Check For Understanding

- Which factors best explain this pattern?
- Is the pattern random, clumped, or uniform?
- What additional measurements would strengthen your claim?

## Common Misconceptions To Address

- abundance and distribution mean the same thing
- biotic interactions are less important than abiotic factors in all cases
- one-time observations prove long-term ecological trends

## Teaching Sequence (Suggested)

1. Start with retrieval questions from prior lessons.
2. Model one example step by step using scientific vocabulary.
3. Run guided practice with immediate feedback.
4. Move to independent application.
5. End with an exit task that requires evidence-based reasoning.

## Adaptation Notes

- Support: sentence starters, partially completed diagrams, key-term banks.
- Extension: unfamiliar data, compare-and-justify prompts, evaluate limitations.
- Assessment alignment: include one short-response and one extended-response item.
