## Requirements

![Kite antenna activation kit laid out on sand](/assets/images/kite-activation/kit.jpg)

### Antenna System

- ~20 m end-fed wire (40 m band)
- 56:1 balun / transformer
- BNC terminated feedline
- Wide ranging Antenna matching unit (Tuner)

### Lift & Grounding

- Kite with sufficient lift (example used: ~1.6 m²)
- Strong kite line
- Serious ground stake (do not cheap out here)
- Bleed resistor array + ground rod
- Elevated radial
- Carabiners (one large/locking helps)
- Elastic/bungee section for shock absorption

---

## Core Deployment Steps

### Step 1 — Ground Before Launch

Connect a bleed resistor array to a ground rod and elevated radial before the kite goes up.  
Wind-driven static buildup can be substantial. Always ground first.

### Step 2 — Attach the Antenna to the Kite Line

Attach the end-fed wire a few metres below the top of the kite using elastic or bungee and a carabiner.  
This reduces shock loading and prevents strain transfer directly to the connector.

### Step 3 — Secure the Kite

Stake the kite securely using a serious ground anchor.  
Confirm the antenna wire is supported by the line attachment — not the balun connector.  
Add a strain-relief loop to a stake.

### Step 4 — Connect and Test

Connect the feedline to the radio, check SWR, and tune if required.  
The goal is a workable match without relying heavily on a tuner.

### Step 5 — Controlled Retrieval

Run the kite line through a large carabiner and walk it down rather than hauling directly by hand.  
Disconnect the antenna at the attachment point, then continue walking down the kite.

This prevents rope burn and excessive load.

---

## Pitfall 1 — Static Is a Real Threat

Wind-induced static on a 20 m wire can be extreme.  
Always maintain grounding.  

Failure to do so risks damaging the radio front-end.

---

## Pitfall 2 — Mechanical Strain & Wire Wrapping

- Do not run the antenna tightly along the kite string — it can wrap and worsen SWR.
- Instead, fly from one location and run the antenna as a sloper.
- Never let the connector take load — use a loop to a stake.
- Use a genuinely strong stake. Even “heavy duty” dog stakes can tremble.

---

## Why This Works

A lightweight kite can lift a full-length 40 m end-fed quickly and efficiently.  

With proper grounding, strain management, and retrieval control, this becomes a portable, high-performance field deployment option — especially in open coastal environments.
