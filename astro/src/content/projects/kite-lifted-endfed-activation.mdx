## Requirements

![Kite antenna activation kit laid out on sand](/images/projects/kite-activation/kit.jpg)

### Antenna System

- ~20 m end-fed wire (40 m band)
- 56:1 balun / transformer
- BNC terminated feedline
- Wide ranging Antenna matching unit (Tuner)

### Lift & Grounding

- Kite with sufficient lift (example used: ~1.6 m²)
- Strong kite line
- Serious ground stake (do not cheap out here)
- Bleed resistor array + ground rod
- Elevated radial
- Carabiners (one large/locking helps)
- Elastic/bungee section for shock absorption

---

## Core Deployment Steps

### Step 1 — Ground Before Launch

Connect a bleed resistor array to a ground rod and elevated radial before the kite goes up.  
Wind-driven static buildup can be substantial. Always ground first.

### Step 2 — Attach the Antenna to the Kite Line

Attach the end-fed wire a few metres below the top of the kite using elastic or bungee and a carabiner.  
This reduces shock loading and prevents strain transfer directly to the connector.

### Step 3 — Secure the Kite

Stake the kite securely using a serious ground anchor.  
Confirm the antenna wire is supported by the line attachment — not the balun connector.  
Add a strain-relief loop to a stake.

### Step 4 — Connect and Test

Connect the feedline to the radio, check SWR, and tune if required.  
The goal is a workable match without relying heavily on a tuner.

### Step 5 — Controlled Retrieval

Run the kite line through a large carabiner and walk it down rather than hauling directly by hand.  
Disconnect the antenna at the attachment point, then continue walking down the kite.

This prevents rope burn and excessive load.

---

## Safety & Electrical Protection

Static discharge from a wind-loaded wire can be significant. The grounding system should be treated as functional, not optional.

---

## Site Walk

A kite antenna system is mechanically simple, but small environmental changes can affect behaviour.

---

## Pitfall 1 — Static Is a Real Threat

In dry coastal wind, measurable static potential accumulated within minutes.  
Grounding should be established before connection to the radio and removed last during pack-down.

---

## Pitfall 2 — Mechanical Strain & Wire Wrapping

A failed balun connector in the field ends the activation.

All load paths should terminate in rope, stake, or elastic rather than in RF hardware.

---

## Why This Works

A lightweight kite can lift a full-length 40 m end-fed quickly and efficiently.  

With proper grounding, strain management, and retrieval control, this becomes a portable, high-performance field deployment option — especially in open coastal environments.

## Detailed System Configuration

<img 
  src="/images/projects/kite-efhw-system.svg" 
  alt="Integrated kite-lifted EFHW system diagram showing load path, RF chain, and static grounding" 
/>

### Antenna

- End-fed half-wave tuned for resonance at 7.074 MHz when deployed as a sloper from approximately 9 m down to 2 m.
- 56:1 autotransformer (homebrew).
- Transformer core configuration: FT82-43 with an FT50-43 nested inside.
- PCB: UniBalun from DG1JAN.
- Wire: Insulated SOTABeams wire.

When deployed as a conventional EFHW (non-kite), this antenna presents a flat SWR below 1.5:1 on all resonant bands and approaches 1:1 on 15 m.

### Counterpoise / Radial

- Elevated radial length: 7.8 m.
- Height above ground: approximately 1.5–2 m.
- Elevated section supported by a tree.

The radial was not bonded to the ground rod. The bleed path to ground was kept physically short (< 30 cm) from the resistor enclosure to the rod.

### Feedline and RF Chain

Signal path:

Antenna wire + elevated radial  
→ Bleed resistor array  
→ Homebrew choke (RG58 wound on FT140-43)  
→ AT-100M Pro tuner  
→ FX-4CR

- Feedline: 5.6 m RG-58 CU.
- Bleed resistor array: 5 × 1 MΩ resistors in parallel (homebuilt), housed in a sealed enclosure with SO-239 connectors.
- Effective resistance: 200 kΩ.

The bleed assembly is permanent and built specifically for field use.

---

## Grounding and Static Behaviour

- Ground rod: 1.3 m steel rod.
- Location: Main beach at Coochiemudlo Island (sand).
- Static potential observed: > 3 kV.

Static accumulation occurred rapidly in coastal wind conditions. No resistance-to-ground measurements were taken during this activation.

The bleed resistor array successfully prevented static discharge into the radio. Further measurement under varying humidity and wind conditions would be useful.

---

## Kite System

- Kite: Rainbow LS-17.5 Lifter Sled (Skydog), purchased from kitesmart.com.au.
- Line: Dyneema rated for 200 kg.
- Wind speed: < 15 kph sustained, gusts < 25 kph.
- Maximum height achieved: ~22 m.
- Sloper angle ranged from ~60° down to ~25° depending on wind.

The antenna attachment point was approximately 2 m below the kite’s line attachment, using a loop and a stretch bungee between kite line and antenna wire.

The kite crashed once during operation (mid-QSO with VK3PF), reinforcing the need for mechanical separation between RF components and load-bearing line.

---

## Electrical Performance in the Field

SWR behaviour differed significantly from fixed-height EFHW operation:

- SWR fluctuated between ~1.0 and >15.
- No stable resonant frequency observed.
- Resonance shifted with wind variation and height changes.
- The AT-100M Pro tuner was required continuously.

Chokes were effective in preventing RF feedback into the operating position.

Noise floor was notably low, likely due to beach location and absence of nearby infrastructure.

---

## Mechanical Observations

Wire wrapping around the kite line was initially a problem. This was mitigated by:

- Tying the antenna lower on the line.
- Ensuring the kite string bore primary tension.
- Preventing any load transfer to the balun connector.

The kite was anchored to a heavy-duty dog stake rated for large breeds. Under gust load, the stake visibly flexed, indicating significant line tension.

---

## Retrieval Method

The “carabiner walk-down” method involved clipping the line to a belt-mounted carabiner. As the operator walked downwind toward the anchor, the carabiner functioned as a controlled redirect, reducing hand load and managing descent speed.

This reduced rope burn risk and provided smoother tension management during recovery.

---

## Environmental and Operational Factors

Unexpected variables included:

- Kite crashes (including interference from a dog attempting to catch it).
- Wind variability affecting both angle and loading.
- Rapid SWR changes with height variation.
- Significant static accumulation.

Despite these factors, deployment and pack-down were straightforward in the open beach environment.

Absolute rule maintained: no flying over people.
